Data confidence and methodological comparison

Consolidated confidence table

Page inspired by the "Data Confidence Assessment" format of the Kenya account (Gacutan et al. 2026), which consolidates the confidence level of every component in a single place — a practice judged exemplary and reused here, applied to the status nomenclature specific to the OASIS-M pipeline (comptabilite-oceanique §0.1): Publishable, Pilot, Proxy — uncalibrated, To be resolved, Blocked, Discarded.

Of the 35 datasets and outputs recorded in the July 2026 inventory, most remain at Pilot, Proxy, or Blocked status. That is consistent with a first-year pilot: sufficient for documented methodological and policy engagement, insufficient for formal adoption into national statistics without targeted reinforcement — the same reading the Kenya account explicitly applies to its own results.

Methodological comparison — OASIS-M vs. Kenya Coastal Ecosystem Accounts

Benchmark note prepared 30 July 2026, comparing point by point the OASIS-M pipeline and the published Kenya Coastal Ecosystem Accounts (Kilifi County, Gacutan et al., March 2026)[^1]. Both accounts share the same IUCN GET typology (M1.3), the same CI normalisation formula, and an almost identical extent/condition/services architecture — making this a direct comparison rather than a regional curiosity.

Element Observation Verdict
Extent account (EC1) Kenya publishes a complete mangrove account (opening/additions/reductions/closing) but no documented precision targets. OASIS has no executed reef result yet but documents quantified precision targets (OA ≥ 75%, confusion matrix on 50–100 field points). Adopt Kenya's presentation format; retain and publish OASIS's precision targets.
Condition composite (EC3) Both accounts independently converge on the same reference levels (COTS 15 ind/ha, Dulvy 2021; coral recruitment 1–3/m², Hughes 2010) — a signal of external robustness. Kenya's mangrove account aggregates 5 indicators by an uncapped simple average (potential bug: CI > 1 possible on DBH/height). OASIS weights by pillar (40/20/40) and systematically checks that every CI stays within [0,1]. Retain and generalise OASIS's [0,1] range assertion; document the Dulvy/Hughes convergence as regional WIO validation.
Services (EC4) Kenya documents provisioning alone (resource rent, 3 rent-share scenarios), with no separate nursery account, and a fixed undeclared-catch factor (1.3x) not anchored to a local CPUE. OASIS has both nursery and provisioning with an anti-double-counting rule and a ΔV = ΔV_CI + ΔV_extent + ΔV_price decomposition. OASIS's architecture is worth retaining as a methodological differentiator.
Asset account (EC2) Kenya has no published asset account (no Table 7.1). OASIS has a pilot EC2 (NPV, depreciation decomposition) but relies on a service value and reef area that are still proxies. A real structural lead, to be treated with the same caution Kenya applies to its own provisional figures — do not cite outside the pilot context.
Causal effect of protected areas Kenya asserts that protection status is "the strongest single predictor of condition" based solely on a comparison of means, with no confidence intervals, tests, effect sizes, or confounder controls. OASIS explicitly requires robust descriptive inference followed by a quasi-experimental approach (matching, regression discontinuity). The most directly exportable point of the OASIS pipeline. Qualify any Kenya efficacy claim as associative, not causal, in any external citation.
Transparency Kenya has a single, consolidated "Data Confidence Assessment" page — an exemplary practice. OASIS has the substantive equivalent but scattered across skills. Adopt the single-synthesis format — this is the purpose of this page.

Recommendations retained for the rest of the OASIS-M pipeline

  1. Keep this cross-cutting synthesis page up to date with every major update to an upstream account (EC1 to EC4).
  2. Add a systematic [0,1] range assertion to every new CI formula (including for peripheral ecosystems).
  3. Document the convergence of reference levels with Kenya (Dulvy 2021, Hughes 2010) as a regional WIO robustness argument, citable in publication.
  4. Explicitly qualify as associative, not causal, any protected-area efficacy claim citing the Kenya accounts as an external reference.
  5. Maintain the same caution discipline on OASIS EC2/MECA figures that Kenya applies to its own provisional accounts.
